 Title: Marketing Launch Pack

Intro:
This pack helps marketers launch products with stronger messaging, cleaner campaign structure, and better execution across ads, landing pages, social content, and audience targeting. Each prompt is practical enough to use in real launch work, not just brainstorming.

Section: Ad Copy

1. Primary Ad Copy Generator
Prompt:
You are a direct-response copywriter. Write 10 primary text ads for [PRODUCT] targeting [AUDIENCE]. Focus on one core pain point, one key outcome, and one clear call to action in each version.

2. Short Paid Social Variations
Prompt:
You are a performance marketer. Create 20 short ad copy variations for [PRODUCT] for Meta or TikTok. Keep each version punchy, clear, and easy to test across different creative angles.

3. Benefit-Led Ad Copy
Prompt:
You are a conversion-focused marketer. Write 8 ad variations for [PRODUCT] where each version leads with a different customer benefit. Keep the tone modern and specific.

4. Objection-Handling Ad Copy
Prompt:
You are a paid acquisition specialist. Create ad copy for [PRODUCT] that directly addresses objections about price, trust, effort, or results. Keep the copy persuasive without sounding defensive.

5. Creative Angle Testing Prompt
Prompt:
You are a growth marketer. Generate 12 ad concepts for [PRODUCT] using these angle types: urgency, curiosity, transformation, proof, simplicity, and comparison. For each angle, include a headline, short body copy, and CTA.

Section: Product Launch

6. Launch Positioning Prompt
Prompt:
You are a senior product marketer. Create a launch positioning statement for [PRODUCT] aimed at [AUDIENCE]. Include the problem, the promised outcome, the differentiator, and a concise positioning line.

7. Launch Plan Builder
Prompt:
You are a launch strategist. Create a 3-week launch plan for [PRODUCT]. Break it into pre-launch, launch week, and post-launch actions. Include priorities, deliverables, and communication goals.

8. Launch Messaging Hierarchy
Prompt:
You are a product messaging consultant. Build a message hierarchy for [PRODUCT] that includes the top value proposition, 3 supporting proof points, 3 audience-specific messages, and a core CTA.

9. Launch Email Sequence
Prompt:
You are an email launch specialist. Draft a 5-email launch sequence for [PRODUCT]. Include subject line, opening hook, core message, urgency or proof element, and final CTA for each email.

10. Offer Stack Builder
Prompt:
You are a sales copy strategist. Create a launch offer for [PRODUCT] including the core product, bonus ideas, urgency mechanism, guarantee angle, and ideal CTA phrasing.

Section: Landing Pages

11. Headline and Subheadline Generator
Prompt:
You are a landing page copywriter. Write 15 headline options and 10 supporting subheadlines for [PRODUCT]. Make them clear, outcome-focused, and tailored to [AUDIENCE].

12. Full Landing Page Outline
Prompt:
You are a conversion copy expert. Build a landing page outline for [PRODUCT] including hero section, pain points, benefits, proof, objections, offer, FAQ, and CTA placement.

13. Benefit Section Writer
Prompt:
You are a marketing writer. Turn these product features into benefit-driven landing page copy. Explain what the feature is, why it matters, and what practical result it gives the customer.

14. FAQ and Objection Prompt
Prompt:
You are a conversion strategist. Generate 12 frequently asked questions for a landing page selling [PRODUCT], along with persuasive but honest answers that reduce hesitation and improve clarity.

15. Landing Page Optimization Review
Prompt:
You are a CRO consultant. Review this landing page draft and suggest improvements for message clarity, CTA strength, proof placement, scannability, and objection handling.

Section: Social Media

16. Launch Content Calendar
Prompt:
You are a social media strategist. Create a 14-day social content calendar for launching [PRODUCT] on [PLATFORMS]. Include post concept, hook, content format, and CTA for each day.

17. Social Hooks Pack
Prompt:
You are a short-form content writer. Write 25 social media hooks for [PRODUCT] across launch teasers, educational posts, proof posts, and direct promotion. Keep them sharp and scroll-stopping.

18. Thread and Carousel Prompt
Prompt:
You are a content marketer. Create a detailed carousel outline or thread for [TOPIC RELATED TO PRODUCT]. Include the opening hook, slide-by-slide points, and final CTA to the offer.

19. Comment-to-Conversion Replies
Prompt:
You are a community manager. Write 12 reply templates for common launch comments and objections under posts about [PRODUCT]. Make them friendly, concise, and aligned with the brand voice.

20. Repurposing Planner
Prompt:
You are a content repurposing strategist. Turn one long-form launch asset into 10 social media posts across different formats. Include what to post, what angle to lead with, and where it fits in the launch timeline.

Section: Audience Targeting

21. Customer Segment Breakdown
Prompt:
You are a market research analyst. Break the audience for [PRODUCT] into 5 useful segments. For each one, describe their pain points, desired outcomes, buying triggers, and messaging angle.

22. Ideal Customer Profile Prompt
Prompt:
You are a product marketer. Build a clear ideal customer profile for [PRODUCT]. Include role, company or life context, goals, frustrations, objections, and what would make them buy now.

23. Messaging by Segment
Prompt:
You are a positioning strategist. Create separate message angles for 3 audience segments for [PRODUCT]. For each segment, write the main promise, objection to overcome, and best CTA wording.

24. Competitor Switching Prompt
Prompt:
You are a demand generation strategist. Build messaging for customers who currently use [COMPETITOR OR ALTERNATIVE]. Explain what may frustrate them now, what they want instead, and how [PRODUCT] should be positioned.

25. Paid Audience Testing Ideas
Prompt:
You are a paid acquisition strategist. Suggest 10 audience targeting experiments for launching [PRODUCT]. Include demographic, interest, behavior, and pain-point based audience ideas, plus what message each audience should see first.

Closing Note:
Use these prompts consistently to get the best results.
The strongest launches come from repetition, testing, and sharper positioning over time, so revisit these prompts as your product and audience evolve.
